# Day and night

The concept is in `docs/licht.md`.

Day and night (`src/light/`, concept in `docs/licht.md`): `light::DayNight` runs a clock with the game time (24 game hours per its `day_length`, the values' one unless the time-lapse shortened it) and derives the phase, how far it is night and where the sun is (`DayNight::sun`, up from the start of the dawn to the end of the dusk); every change to the time goes through a method taking the `LightValues`, so the derived values always fit. The values come from `assets/light.ron` (`light::values`, hot-reloaded into the `LightValues` resource, which also inserts `DayNight` the first time; tests insert both directly). The night sets the base of the player's `SightRadius`. Colliders with `light::shadow::CastsShadow` (Tiled's `height` in px) are rasterized into each map's `shadow::Heights`, which `ShadowHeights` puts together over the loaded maps whenever they change. `shadow::SunShadow` marks the cells in the sun's shadow by walking from each caster cell away from the sun (up to `shadow::MAX_SHADOW_LENGTH`, which `world::update_chunking` also loads beyond the view), and only again once the tip of the longest shadow moved by a pixel; it is computed per cell on the CPU, since a shader walking per pixel cost 8–14 ms per frame at 4K, where a cell covers hundreds of pixels. `light::render` draws one layer above everything but the HUD (z = 20, following the camera, hidden while there is neither night nor shadow) with its own `LightMaterial` (shader `assets/shaders/light.wesl`): it darkens by the night and by the shadow mask (a linearly filtered texture, sampled four times per pixel for soft edges), the latter only within the sight (it samples the same blurred sight mask as the darkening outside the sight, through the shared `assets/shaders/sight_mask.wesl`, so the two don't stack; without sun it reads neither mask), and is lifted by `render::Lightmap` (a single dark pixel until there are light sources). `light::grading` grades the colors by the time of day: `assets/light.ron` holds a `Grading` per phase (exposure, temperature, tint, saturation, contrast), and `DayNight::grading` blends them (day and night by the night, the dusk's and the dawn's towards the middle of the twilight) into the camera's `ColorGrading`. The camera has `Hdr` for it while `Settings::color_grading` is on (`grade_colors` sets a neutral grading otherwise; MSAA is off by default, since 4× MSAA with HDR doubled the frame time at 4K), so Bevy's tonemapping pass grades the whole image (the tilemap's shader doesn't grade by itself); the output stays SDR, and the HUD is drawn after the pass and keeps its colors. The HUD shows the clock with dev tools.
