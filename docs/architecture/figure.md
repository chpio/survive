# Figures

Figures (`src/figure.rs`) are the animated characters: the player and all enemies, and animated objects like chests (the decision is in `docs/ideen/ideen.md`, section "Grafik"). The game shows them in the oblique view of the tileset (ground from above, walls from the front), in 8 directions.

**Building them.** A figure is modeled in 3D in Blender by a script in `art/` (`art/player.py`, `art/beetle.py`, `art/chest.py`, run headless with `blender -b --factory-startup -P art/<name>.py -- [--preview DIR]`): convex parts (boxes, ellipsoids, capsules) on the bones of an armature, animated once by a pose function per animation. The scripts are the source; the generated `.blend` (to look at in Blender) isn't checked in. `art/figures.py` holds the shared code and exports the figure as `assets/characters/<name>.glb`: for each direction it turns the figure, projects each part into the oblique view (a point (x, y, z) shows at (x, y + z), the larger z - y is in front) and keeps one flat shape per part, the outline of its projection at rest. Per frame, each part is moved, turned and scaled to fit its projection as well as it can, and its depth (plus a bias per part, for parts whose centers are equally deep) becomes its z within 0..0.001. The preview is an animated GIF per animation with all directions, drawn from the same data. Conventions: 1 Blender unit is 1 px, the feet stand at the origin, where the game's collider is, the figure faces +X, Z is up.

**The file.** The `.glb` has a top node `figure` with a child per direction (`d0` faces +X, counterclockwise from there), each holding that direction's parts as flat meshes, and the animations `<name>/<direction>`. Bevy's own glTF loader would pull in its 3D renderer, so `figure::FigureLoader` reads it with the `gltf` crate: `figure::parse` turns it into plain data (nodes with parents before children, parts with color and vertices, keyframes per node and property; the tests use it directly), and the loader makes a `figure::Figure` asset with a mesh per part, an `AnimationClip` per animation and direction, and one `AnimationGraph`. Every node may only rotate around Z and keep its Z scale; the loader rejects other files.

**Showing them.** `FigureRoot(handle)` on an entity (with `Transform` and `Visibility`, the unit's `Body` child) shows the figure as its children; `FigureControl` (required) says which animation plays, where the figure faces, how fast the animation runs and whether it loops (`repeat`; otherwise it stops on its last frame, and with speed 0 it holds its first). With `Pickable` on the root, its parts get `Pickable` too, and clicks on them bubble up to the root. `figure::update` spawns only the parts of the direction closest to `facing` (`direction_for`, which holds the current direction until `facing` is a few degrees past the border, so figures walking between two directions don't flicker), and spawns the other direction when it changes, going on with the animation where it was. Each part is a `Mesh2d` with a `SightMaterial` in its color (one material per color). Saving a `.glb` again rebuilds its figures (hot reload).

- Sight: the parts carry a `MeshTag` with the rounded position of the figure's feet (`anchor_tag`), and the sight shader samples the mask there instead of at each pixel, so a figure fades in and out as a whole, judged where it stands, and a head above a wall doesn't follow the mask of the ground behind it. Meshes without a tag (items, the darkening) still sample at each pixel.
- Sorting: `update` sets the root's z by its height on screen (`sorted_z`, 0..0.9 within the unit's z of 10; lower is in front, 1 px apart is 0.001 apart, more than a figure's parts span). Units aren't sorted against the map's objects yet; they are drawn above all map layers.
- Health bars sit above `combat::health_bar::UnitHeight`, the height of the figure above the unit's center.

All enemies are beetles, scaled by their radius over 5 px; `enemy::face_heading` turns `FigureControl::facing` towards the `Heading` at `TURN_SPEED`, and `enemy::animate_walk` runs the walk animation as fast as the enemy moves and slower for larger enemies (normal pace at 40 px/s times the scale, at most four times that), so it stops with the enemy. The player's figure (`player::body`) faces where the player wants to walk and plays `walk` while moving (pace 80 px/s), `idle` while standing.
