# Combat and swarm enemies

The concept is in `docs/schwarm.md`.

Combat (`src/combat/`): units with `Health` and an `Attack` hit every opponent whose collider is within the attack's reach once per cooldown (the player all enemies around it, an awake enemy the player). A hit deals damage and knocks the target back with an impulse, so lighter units fly further. A hit that takes the last health triggers the entity event `combat::Died` on the target, and the objects' observers decide what dying means: `enemy::despawn_on_death` despawns the enemy, `player::start_over_on_death` removes all enemies and heals the player. The combat and swarm tests add these observers themselves, since the objects' plugins need rendering and input. `combat::Plugin` also applies the healing of used items (`items::ItemUsed` with `Effect::Heal`, capped at the maximum). `player::combat()` and `Enemy::combat` hold the values. `combat::health_bar` draws sprite bars above damaged units and is kept apart from the logic, since it needs rendering.

Swarm enemies (`src/objects/enemy/swarm/`, concept in `docs/schwarm.md`): `spawn_enemy` marks enemies up to `swarm::MAX_RADIUS` (the small ones) with `Swarm`, which `move_towards_player` leaves out. While awake they have a `SwarmState` (`Circle` around the player at their `Orbit`, `Approach` while holding an attack token, `Retreat` to a point to the side, relative to the player) and lose it with their token when they fall asleep. Attack tokens (`combat::tokens`) cap how many units attack the player at once: `AttackToken` is a component of its holder, so despawning gives it back; the swarm hands free tokens to the enemies circling longest and takes them back `token_time` after the approach began (`swarm::TokenHold`), so each token allows at most one hit per that time. `combat::Restrained` units (required by `Swarm`) only attack while `combat::Striking`, which a swarm enemy is during its approach. `combat::Hit` reports every hit, which ends an approach (the enemy's own or the player's). Both kinds of enemies steer apart through `enemy::Crowd`. The values come from `assets/swarm.ron` (`SwarmValues`, loaded by `swarm::values`, which also sets `AttackTokens`; tests insert both), the randomness from `SwarmRng` (`fastrand`, seeded in tests). The swarm's commands use `try_insert`/`try_remove`, since combat may despawn the enemy in the same frame.
